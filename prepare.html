What to Expect

Your Himalayan journey begins weeks before you board the plane. Here's a day-by-day breakdown of a typical Everest Base Camp experience.

Pre
Before You Arrive — Preparation
Get your Nepal e-visa. Book travel insurance with helicopter evacuation cover. Start a fitness routine 12 weeks out. Consult your doctor about Diamox. Purchase or rent gear. Pack smart — teahouse porters carry max 15kg.
1
Arrival in Kathmandu (1,400m)
Your guide meets you at Tribhuvan Airport. Transfer to hotel in Thamel. Gear check meeting — rental gear pickup if needed. Briefing dinner: route overview, permits, health check. Rest well for the Lukla flight.
2
The Lukla Flight (2,860m) — World's Most Dramatic Airport
30-minute Twin Otter flight through mountain valleys. Lukla's sloped runway ends in a cliff — breathtaking but perfectly safe. Trek begins immediately through pine forests. You'll feel the altitude — take it slow, stay hydrated.
3–5
Namche Bazaar (3,440m) — Acclimatization
Spend 2 nights at Namche. Crucial acclimatization period. Expect headaches, reduced appetite, vivid dreams — this is normal. Day hike to 3,880m without sleeping there. Saturday market is a highlight. WiFi, hot showers, good espresso (seriously).
6–8
Into the High Khumbu (4,000–4,500m)
Trails thin. Yak caravans share the path. Tengboche Monastery evening prayers are unforgettable. Air gets noticeably thinner. Breathing becomes deliberate. Sleep may be disrupted — earplugs help in teahouses. Your appetite drops; eat anyway.
9–11
High Camp Zone (4,500–5,364m)
Lobuche. Gorak Shep. Thin air, cold nights (-15°C possible), stunning silence. Your guide monitors you daily for AMS symptoms. The EBC approach crosses glacier moraine — slow, methodical, exhilarating. Base Camp itself is anticlimactic for some — but Kalapathar sunrise never disappoints.
12+
Descent & Return to Kathmandu
Descent is fast and joyful — your body loves the thickening air. You'll notice your energy returning with every 100m of descent. Celebration in Namche. Final Lukla flight. Back in Kathmandu for a celebration dinner. You did it.
The Golden Rule of Altitude
"Walk high, sleep low." Acclimatization day hikes always go higher than your sleeping altitude. Never skip rest days. Never push through AMS symptoms. Your guide has final authority on safety decisions — trust them completely.

Altitude Sickness — Know Before You Go

Altitude sickness (acute mountain sickness / AMS) affects everyone differently. Even the fittest athletes can be struck. Understanding the symptoms and knowing when to act is the most important safety knowledge you can have.

Mild AMS
Headache · Fatigue · Dizziness · Loss of appetite · Nausea · Poor sleep

Action: Rest at current altitude. Don't ascend until symptoms resolve. Take ibuprofen/paracetamol.
Moderate AMS
Severe headache · Persistent vomiting · Extreme fatigue · Shortness of breath at rest · Confusion

Action: DESCEND immediately. Take Diamox. Alert your guide. Do not wait for morning.
HACE / HAPE (Severe)
Ataxia (can't walk straight) · Gurgling in chest · Blue lips · Cannot stay conscious

Action: MEDICAL EMERGENCY. Descend immediately. Helicopter evacuation. Your guide activates insurance immediately.
The Acclimatization Rule
Above 3,000m, never ascend more than 300–500m of sleeping altitude per day. For every 1,000m gained, take one rest day. Your itinerary is built around this — do not try to rush ahead.

Prevention Tips

Do
Hydrate 3–4L of water daily · Eat even when not hungry · Rest on acclimatization days · Tell your guide any symptoms immediately · Take Diamox if prescribed · Walk slowly ("Sherpa pace")
Don't
Don't push through headaches · Don't ascend with symptoms · Don't drink alcohol above 3,500m · Don't take sleeping pills (suppress breathing) · Don't hide symptoms from your guide

Diamox (Acetazolamide) — The Complete Guide

Diamox is a carbonic anhydrase inhibitor that helps your body acclimatize by stimulating breathing. It's widely used by trekkers and is available over-the-counter in Kathmandu pharmacies.

Diamox at a Glance
125–250mg
Typical Dose

Frequency
24–48 hrs
Before Ascent
~$2 USD
Cost in Kathmandu
Who Should Consider Diamox
• History of altitude sickness
• Fast ascent itinerary (14 days to EBC)
• Individual susceptibility (genetics)
• Peace of mind on your once-in-a-lifetime trek
Contraindications (Do Not Take If)
• Sulfa drug allergy
• Kidney disease
• Pregnant or breastfeeding
• Taking certain blood thinners
Always consult your doctor first!
Where to Buy in Kathmandu
Diamox is available without prescription at most pharmacies in Thamel, Kathmandu at a fraction of Western prices. Your guide will take you to a trusted pharmacy during your arrival briefing. Bring a note from your doctor if possible. Common brand names: Acetazolamide, Diamox, Azetamide.

Note: Diamox causes increased urination (helpful) and tingling in fingers/toes (harmless). Carbonated drinks may taste flat. Side effects are generally mild and resolve on stopping the medication. It does NOT replace proper acclimatization — it's a supplement to good planning.

Travel Insurance — Non-Negotiable

Travel insurance with helicopter evacuation coverage is mandatory for all Peak Pursuit treks above 5,000m. A helicopter rescue from EBC costs $5,000–$15,000+ without insurance. This is not optional.

Why Helicopter Evac Coverage Matters
At 5,000m+ there are no roads. The only way out in an emergency is helicopter. HACE and HAPE can turn fatal within hours. Your insurance must explicitly cover helicopter evacuation above 5,000m (many standard policies exclude this). Some policies require pre-authorization — your guide will handle this.

What to Check in Your Policy
1. Does it cover helicopter evacuation above 5,000m specifically? 2. What is the maximum altitude covered? 3. Is there a pre-authorization requirement (call before evac)? 4. What's the medical expense limit ($100,000+ recommended)? 5. Does it cover trip cancellation due to AMS?

Break in your boots completely — blisters can end a trek
Week 12: TAPER — reduce intensity, prioritize sleep and nutrition

Nepal Visa & Trekking Permits

All international trekkers require a Nepal tourist visa plus two trekking permits for the Sagarmatha (Everest) National Park region.

Nepal Tourist Visa
Available on arrival at Tribhuvan International Airport (Kathmandu) or via e-visa online.

Cost: 15 days $30 · 30 days $50 · 90 days $125
Process: ~30 mins at airport. Have passport photos and USD cash ready.
TIMS Card
Trekkers' Information Management System card — required for all trekkers.

Cost: ~$20 USD
Where: Nepal Tourism Board, Kathmandu. Your guide arranges this for you.
Sagarmatha National Park Permit
Required for all EBC-region treks. Entry to the national park.

Cost: ~$30 USD (included in your Peak Pursuit package)
Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality
Local municipality fee recently introduced for all Khumbu trekkers.

Cost: ~$8 USD (included in your package)
Note: Permits are checked at multiple checkpoints on trail.

Kathmandu Arrival Tips

Your adventure starts the moment you land. Here's what to expect in Kathmandu and how to use your pre-trek time wisely.

✈️
At the Airport
Your Peak Pursuit guide or driver meets you in the arrivals hall with a sign. Do NOT take unofficial taxi offers. The drive to Thamel is 20–40 minutes. ATMs are available at the airport — withdraw NPR (Nepali Rupees) and USD.
💊
Day 1 in Kathmandu
Gear check with your guide. Visit pharmacy for Diamox if needed. Currency exchange at hotel rate (or Thamel money changers). Brief walk around Thamel — the adventure hub with dozens of gear shops. Early dinner and rest.
🕌
Optional: Sightseeing Day
If you have extra time, visit Pashupatinath Temple, Boudhanath Stupa (the world's largest Buddhist stupa), or Swayambhunath (Monkey Temple). Your guide can arrange a half-day tour. Acclimatize at Kathmandu's 1,400m before ascending to Lukla.
